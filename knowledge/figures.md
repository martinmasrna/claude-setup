**A figure that reads as a table gets rejected.** ("Neither of your designs was a graph, they were fucking tables.") The reader at a figure wants the mechanism they can't see, not a second rendering of numbers they just read — a visual restating an adjacent code block earns nothing. Ask what the reader is *feeling* at that point in the piece and what motion or mechanism answers it, before choosing a mark; prefer flow/area/position forms over grids. State each fact exactly once: kill captions that explain what the figure shows and labels that tell the reader what it means ("drop the AI slop label"). A heading set in the same case as its items is just a bigger item — capitalise it instead of reaching for weight. Build the alternative and show it rather than describing it; Martin judges visually and reacts fast.

**Judge at reading size, by eye.** A 1200px canvas reviewed at 1200px shipped ~7px titles into an X article column (renders at ~0.53 desktop, ~0.32 phone) — Martin caught it from a screenshot, not me. And arithmetic predictions failed in both directions (2 of 2 wrong): short bold labels on high-contrast fills survive far below the nominal size floor, thin dark text on white does not. So: render the figure in a box the width of its real column (~650px for an X article, ~390px phone) and *look*, on the first draft, not at the end. Essential payload text needs ~20px on a 1200px canvas, but confirm by eye. Never propose a type change from computed effective-pixel numbers alone.
