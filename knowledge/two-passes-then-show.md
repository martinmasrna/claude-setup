Figures session, 2026-08-12/13, measured from the git trail rather than
remembered. `hidden-dial-fanout.py` took **9 commits in 33 minutes** (`80f61b7`
22:04 → `fca588a` 22:37) — palette,
label column, section headings, ribbons, enclosure edge, enclosure padding, row
rhythm, section rules, a dead constant. **Martin commented on none of it.** The
one feedback-driven change to that figure landed 20 hours later, when he asked
it to share the pills' palette.

`system-map.py` is the control: 8 commits, 6 of them traceable to a specific
message from him (rainbow order, 1→7 sequencing, brackets, line counts, type
size). He approved that figure and picked it over its competitor.

Same session, same shape at the portfolio level: **10 figures built, 5 shipped.**

**Why:** polishing feels like progress and is fully self-authorising — no one has
to reply for it to continue. But he is the only grader, and the record shows his
input converged the work while my own taste loop mostly moved pixels he never
saw. This is the cost side of [[two-passes-then-show]]: building wide options is
right, but each option only needs to be good enough to *judge*, not finished.

**How to apply:** two self-directed passes on any artifact, then show it — a
render, a proof page, a message. If I'm about to start a third pass without
having shown anything, that's the signal to ship it for judgement instead.

[[figures]] (am I even judging it at the size he'll see it?).
