Figures session, 2026-08-13. I built a 1,290-vs-13 unit chart for the MatrAIx
piece. Martin: *"basic and amateurish."* I rebuilt it as a single dense slab with
strata and a proper ramp — genuinely better drawing. He then cut it outright:
*"It makes the mini matrix look small, and not interesting. Maybe we can just do
without it?"* The figure's punchline was **ours is one percent the size of
theirs**, which argues against an article whose whole proposition is *you can
build this yourself over a weekend*. I heard "amateurish", fixed the craft, and
never asked whether the figure should exist. **The rebuild made it worse**,
because a well-made figure undercuts the thesis more effectively than a clumsy
one.

Same session, same shape: I drew three traits sitting unattached to pose a
question section 3 would answer. He cut them — a reader who assumes the figure is
broken is lost for the section, and the move depended on them choosing to wonder.

**Why:** "this looks bad" and "this is doing the wrong job" produce the same
first reaction from him, and craft is the easier one to act on. Polishing is
motion that feels like progress while the actual defect survives. Related to
[[figures]], but distinct: that one is about the *form* being
wrong, this one about a well-formed figure making the wrong *argument*.

**How to apply:** on any rejection of a figure, ask first — what does this figure
argue, and does the piece want that argued here? Only then touch the palette,
the layout, the type. When rebuilding, say what the figure claims in one sentence
and check it against the article's proposition; if they fight, propose cutting it
rather than redrawing it. Cheap to test: he will tell you straight away.
